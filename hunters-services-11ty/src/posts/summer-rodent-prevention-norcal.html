When most folks picture rodent season, they think of cold winter nights. Here in Northern California, though, summer brings its own quiet rush of mice and rats looking for a new place to call home. As the hills around Oroville, Chico, Paradise, and Redding dry out and the heat climbs, rodents start searching for the same things we all want in July: cool shade, a steady water source, and a calm place to rest. Very often, that search leads them straight to our garages, attics, and crawl spaces.

Why Summer Draws Rodents Indoors

Rodents are creatures of comfort. When outdoor temperatures spike and natural water sources shrink, a home's shaded attic or a dripping garden hose becomes very appealing. Ripening fruit trees, full bird feeders, and pet food left on the patio all add to the invitation. Mice can slip through a gap the size of a dime, and rats need only about a half inch, so even a tidy home can have a few overlooked entry points.

Start With a Walk Around Your Home

One of the most helpful things you can do is a slow, honest lap around your house. Look where utility lines and pipes enter the wall, check the corners of your garage door, and peek at vents and weep holes. Small gaps near the foundation, worn weather stripping, and torn attic screens are the usual suspects. Sealing these with sturdy materials like steel wool packed into copper mesh or proper hardware cloth makes a real difference, because rodents struggle to chew through metal.

Take Away the Food and Water

Rodents rarely stay where there is nothing to eat or drink. Store pet food and birdseed in sealed containers, pick up fallen fruit from your yard, and keep outdoor grills and eating areas clean. Fix leaky spigots and hoses, and empty any standing water that collects in saucers or buckets. When you remove the buffet, you remove much of the reason for them to move in.

Tidy Up the Edges of Your Yard

Summer is also a smart time to trim back the greenery that gives rodents cover and a bridge to your roof. Keep tree limbs a few feet away from the house, trim shrubs off the foundation, and clear woodpiles and clutter away from exterior walls. This bit of yard care does double duty in NorCal, since a clean, well-managed landscape also supports the defensible space our foothill communities rely on during fire season.
