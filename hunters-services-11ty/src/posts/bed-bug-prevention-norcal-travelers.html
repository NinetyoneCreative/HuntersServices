Between road trips through the foothills, flights out of Sacramento, and stays at hotels or vacation rentals, a lot of us are living out of a suitcase right about now. What most travelers don't think about is that bed bugs can hitch a ride home in that same suitcase, and they're just as happy to settle into a house in Oroville or Chico as they are a hotel room anywhere else.

It's Not About Cleanliness

One of the first things we tell homeowners is that bed bugs have nothing to do with how clean a home is. They're expert hitchhikers that travel in luggage, backpacks, used furniture, and even folded clothing. A five-star hotel and a roadside motel can both pick them up, and so can the tidiest home in town. If you ever find yourself dealing with them, there's no reason to feel embarrassed. It happens to careful, clean households all the time, and it's simply a matter of taking a few sensible steps.

Before You Unpack: Checking Your Hotel Room

A quick habit that pays off is giving any hotel or rental room a once-over before you settle in. Pull back the sheets and check the mattress seams and headboard for tiny reddish-brown insects or small dark spots. Keep your suitcase on the provided luggage rack rather than the bed or floor, and away from upholstered furniture, until you've had a look around. It only takes a couple of minutes and it's a habit worth building into every trip.

On the Way Home

If you're road-tripping through Northern California this summer or flying back into Sacramento, a hard-sided suitcase is a little easier to inspect than a soft duffel bag, since bed bugs have fewer seams and folds to hide in. Either way, try to keep luggage off hotel beds and floors throughout your stay, and give it one more look before you load up the car or check a bag for the flight home.

What to Do Once You're Back

The moment you're home is actually the easiest time to catch a hitchhiker before it settles in. Unpack directly into the washer, and run clothing through a hot dryer cycle for at least 20 minutes, since heat is one of the most effective and simplest tools against bed bugs. Vacuum the inside seams and pockets of your suitcase, then store it somewhere other than under the bed or deep in a closet for a few days while you keep an eye out. It's a small routine, but it makes a real difference.

Signs to Watch For at Home

If bed bugs do make it into your home, you might notice small reddish-brown insects along mattress seams or furniture joints, tiny rust-colored spots on sheets, or a cluster of bites you didn't have the night before. None of these signs mean you did anything wrong. They just mean it's time for a closer look, ideally from someone who knows what to look for.
