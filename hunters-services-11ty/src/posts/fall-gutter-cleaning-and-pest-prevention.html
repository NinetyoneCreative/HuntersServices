Most people think of gutters as roof maintenance. Clean them out, keep the water moving, done. That is true as far as it goes, but by the time a gutter is bad enough to spill visibly down a wall, something else has usually been going on for a while.

Here is what we see across Oroville, Chico, Paradise and Redding every fall. A gutter fills with pine needles, oak leaves and grit off the shingles. Water backs up behind the debris and sits there. It does not pour over the front edge the way you would expect. It creeps backward, under the drip edge, into the fascia board and the roof sheathing behind it. That wood stays damp through every rain from October to April.

Damp wood is the whole story. Carpenter ants do not chew into dry, sound lumber if they can help it. They look for wood that has already softened, and a fascia board that has been wet for months is exactly that. Once they are in the fascia they are inside the roofline, and from the roofline they can reach ceiling joists, wall voids and the kitchen ceiling where you finally notice them.

Termite activity follows the same logic. Subterranean termites need moisture. A downspout that dumps at the base of a wall keeps that soil wet all winter. That puts a constant moisture source right against your foundation and siding. Corners of a house are where downspouts usually land, and that is where the soil stays wet longest.

Then there is the debris itself. A packed gutter is a long, protected, slightly damp trough four inches wide, sitting right at the roofline. Rodents use it as a runway. Wasps and yellowjackets build under the lip. Mosquitoes breed in the standing water once it warms back up in spring. None of that requires a gap in your house. It is all happening on the outside, which is why it goes unnoticed until something comes indoors.

What You Can Check From the Ground

You do not need a ladder to get a read on this. Two things tell you most of what you need to know.

First, look at the fascia board, the flat board the gutter hangs on. Paint that is bubbling, peeling or stained in a vertical streak means water has been sitting behind the gutter, not running through it. Dark staining on the siding directly under a gutter joint means the same thing.

Second, follow each downspout to where it ends. If it empties straight onto soil at the base of the wall, that is a wet corner all winter. An extension or a splash block moves that water three or four feet out from the foundation. It is one of the cheapest improvements you can make to a house in this area.

Timing Matters Here

Mid-September to mid-October is usually the window in Northern California. The oaks and pines have dropped their first round, the rains have not started, and the roof is dry enough to work on safely. Clean it in August and you will do it again. Wait until November and you are on a wet roof solving a problem that already has a two-month head start.

If you have a home in Paradise or Magalia with pines overhead, you may need two cleanouts, one now and one after the needles finish dropping. Homes under valley oaks in Chico and Durham usually get through on one, done late enough to catch the bulk of the leaf drop.
